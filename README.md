# cs-255

- Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
  - The DriverPass project was for a company looking to build a website for their business, but they wanted it to be a pretty sophisticated platform that could perform multiple tasks. They wanted to have practice exams, driver lesson booking, and the ability to download executive summary reports in a .csv format. Their existing system was handled mainly by phone I suspect.
- What did you do particularly well?
  - I focused on getting done what needed to get done, which included a requirements collection document, a business requirements document, multiple diagrams including different types of UML diagrams, as well as a system design document.
- If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
  - I would revise the Technical Requirements section in the system design document and I would add the various technical requirements for the software development team instead of just including requirements for the generic user.
- How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
  - One way of looking at what a company does is considering business needs and one way of looking at business needs is taking into consideration user needs. Use cases are a great way to incorporate user's needs, so I had a UML use case diagram that clearly showed what the needed to be able to do on the DriverPass platform. 
- How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
  - I approach designing software from a top-down perspective starting from the technology stack or computer architecture side of engineering. Then I get into the architectural details of each technology or framework in the tech stack, and from there I try to learn all the details of the technology or framework. Now, from this class, I will be able to think visually and think in UML diagrams which go from higher-level to lower-level so I will have visual diagrams to accompany technological details.
